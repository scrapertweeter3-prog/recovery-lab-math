A recovery week is a measurement week

The week you cut training volume in half is usually treated as lost time. It is the opposite: it is the only week where the numbers mean anything. During normal training every recovery signal you own, resting heart rate, grip strength, morning readiness, sleep consistency, is dragged around by fatigue, so you cannot tell what the baseline even is. Deload and the noise drops out. That is the week to measure, and at HackedSelf the whole method is built on exactly that: cheap self-tests, run on a schedule, read against your own history instead of a chart in a study.

The reason baseline matters is that every comparison you will ever make hangs on it. Suppose your resting heart rate sits at 52 during a deload and drifts to 58 mid-block. That 6 beat rise, 11.5 percent, is a training-load conversation. If it drifts to 58 during the deload itself, it is a sickness or stress conversation instead. Same number, opposite verdicts, and the only thing that separates them is knowing where your own floor sits. Grip strength works the same way: a 5 percent drop off your measured baseline is a fatigue signal worth acting on, while a number you have never compared against anything is just trivia.

So the protocol is boring by design. Pick a deload week, take the cheap measurements on two or three mornings in a row, and write them down where you can find them in a year. That record is the asset. Every future decision about training load, sleep debt, or a supplement that claims to help recovery gets checked against numbers you personally collected, on the week your body was actually resting. Evidence-first does not mean reading more studies. It means owning a baseline.
